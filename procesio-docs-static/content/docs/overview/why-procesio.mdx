PROCESIO is a platform for building business processes: the automation itself, the forms people fill in, the approvals, the documents, and the data the process keeps. It is built for the processes where a mistake costs something. A missed filing deadline, a rejected invoice, an order that never reached the ERP. And for processes that have to show, months later, exactly what happened.

This page explains what that means in practice, with a link to the documentation behind each claim.

## Your bill doesn't grow with your success

Most automation platforms charge per task, per operation, per connector or per active workflow. The better your automation works, the more you pay.

PROCESIO charges for processing time: the time your actions are actively executing. Queues and platform orchestration are never billed. Waiting for a person or a timer is free, so a process that sits for three days waiting on an approval costs nothing while it waits.

Plans include unlimited processes, users and workspaces. See [Processing time and usage](/processing-time-and-usage) for how it is measured and [Pricing](https://procesio.com/pricing) for current plans.

## Run it where your data has to stay

| Option | Where it runs |

## Built for processes that can fail

Any process that depends on another system will eventually meet a system that doesn't answer. What matters is whether you find out, and what happens next.

- Every action that can fail has an [Error Path](/error-path), a separate route for the failure, so the process never carries on as if the step had worked.
- [Delay](/how-to/delay) and [Schedules](/schedules) let a process retry inside a time window instead of giving up.
- [Throw](/how-to/throw) stops a run on purpose when the data is wrong, with your own message.
- Every run is recorded: what came in, what went out, which steps ran and how long each took.

## Code in the languages your team already uses

When no ready-made action fits, your team writes code in a language it already knows. [Scripting](/how-to/scripting) actions run JavaScript, Node.js, Python and Ruby inside a process, and [Custom Actions](/docs/custom-actions/what-is-a-custom-action) let developers package their own actions and connectors in C#. Nobody has to learn a vendor-specific mapping language or get certified in one.

## When there's no ready-made connector

PROCESIO has fewer ready-made connectors than the largest platforms, and we would rather say so here than have you find out later. What it has instead:

- [Call API](/how-to/call-api) reaches any REST API, with the credentials stored once and reused across processes.
- The AI Connector Builder writes and compiles a connector from an API's documentation.
- [Custom Actions](/docs/custom-actions/what-is-a-custom-action) cover whatever is left.

## AI with brakes

AI is good at reading messy input: a free-text email, a scanned invoice, a contract. It is not safe to let it act alone on money or on a compliance filing. PROCESIO lets you use both halves:

- [AI Decisional](/how-to/ai-decisional) routes a process using conditions written in plain English.
- Deterministic checks validate what the model returned before anything is written anywhere.
- [Approval](/approval) puts a person in front of every case the checks can't settle.

You bring your own model key, and there is no markup on AI usage.

## Operable from outside

Everything you build in the interface is also available programmatically: the [PROCESIO API](/docs/developers-guide/procesio-api-documentation), [API Keys](/api-keys) for running processes from other systems, and procesio-cli for creating, validating, running and exporting processes from scripts or from AI agents.

## Governance built in

[User permissions and roles](/user-permissions-and-roles-management), [two-factor authentication](/two-factor-authentication) that an administrator can require for everyone in a master workspace, and [Resource Tracking](/resource-tracking) to see what every process consumes.

## Leaving is your right

The EU Data Act, which has applied since 12 September 2025, gives customers of cloud and data-processing services the right to switch providers, and the transition that still allows switching charges ends on 12 January 2027. We don't depend on lock-in. Your processes export as files, and your data is yours.

## When another tool fits better

- **A simple link between two popular apps**, with no state to keep and no deadline to meet. A task-based tool will do it quickly and cheaply.
- **Clicking through desktop screens.** PROCESIO doesn't automate desktop user interfaces. When a step only exists on a screen, an RPA tool can do that one step while PROCESIO runs the process around it.

Saying this early saves both sides a project that would struggle by its third month.
