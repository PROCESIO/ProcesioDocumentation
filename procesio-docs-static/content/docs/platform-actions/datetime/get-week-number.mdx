description: "Returns the week of the year a date falls in, using the week rules you choose."
---

**Get Week Number** returns the number of the week of the year that a date falls in, for example `46`. Use it for weekly reports, weekly folders or to group records by week. You choose which day starts the week and how the first week of the year is counted.

## Add the action [#add-the-action]
In the Process Designer toolbox, open **DateTime** and pick **Get Week Number** (or type *Get Week Number* in the search box).

![The Get Week Number action properties: Date, First Week Rule, First Day Of Week and Week Number](/images/docs/actions/datetime-get-week-number/panel.png)

## Inputs and outputs [#inputs-and-outputs]
| Field | Direction | Type | Required | What to put / what you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Date** | Input | Date and time | Yes | The date to get the week number for. |
| **First Week Rule** | Input | Option list | Yes | How week 1 of the year is chosen. See the options below. |
| **First Day Of Week** | Input | Option list | Yes | **Sunday** or **Monday**: the day each week starts on. |
| **Week Number** | Output | Integer | Yes | The week of the year, from `1` to `53` (`54` in rare cases with **FirstDay**). |

**First Week Rule** options:
- **FirstDay**: week 1 is the week that contains 1 January, however few days of it are in the new year.
- **FirstFullWeek**: week 1 starts on the first **First Day Of Week** of the year. Days before it belong to the last week of the previous year.
- **FirstFourDayWeek**: week 1 is the first week that has at least four days in the new year.

## Example [#example]
| Date | First Week Rule | First Day Of Week | Week Number |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `2026-11-10` | FirstDay | Sunday | `46` |
| `2026-11-10` | FirstFourDayWeek | Monday | `46` |
| `2027-01-01` (a Friday) | FirstDay | Monday | `1` |
| `2027-01-01` | FirstFullWeek | Monday | `52` |
| `2027-01-01` | FirstFourDayWeek | Monday | `53` |
| `2024-12-30` (a Monday) | FirstFourDayWeek | Monday | `53` |

## Good to know [#good-to-know]
- The result is always counted inside the calendar year of the date. Early-January and late-December dates can get `52` or `53`.
- **FirstFourDayWeek** with **Monday** is close to the ISO 8601 week, but not the same at the end of the year: ISO puts `2024-12-30` in week 1 of 2025, this action returns `53`. Check the last days of December if you need strict ISO weeks.
- A date with a UTC offset is first converted to UTC. An empty **Date** stops the step with an error.
