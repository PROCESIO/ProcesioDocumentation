**For:** groups with legal entities in several EU countries, and the finance and tax teams who run their invoicing.

## The problem today

Each EU country is bringing in its own e-invoicing mandate, with its own format, its own network and its own timetable. A group with entities in Belgium, Poland, France and Germany ends up with a different provider or ERP module per country. Nobody can see in one place which invoices were accepted and which are stuck.

| Country | Status in November 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Belgium | Domestic B2B e-invoicing mandatory since 1 January 2026, over Peppol |
| Poland | Receiving through KSeF mandatory since 1 February 2026. Issuing mandatory from 1 February 2026 for taxpayers whose 2024 sales exceeded PLN 200 million, and from 1 April 2026 for the rest; those invoicing no more than PLN 10,000 a month have until 1 January 2027 |
| France | Every company must be able to receive from 1 September 2026; large and mid-sized companies issue from that date, smaller ones from 1 September 2027 |
| Germany | Receiving mandatory since 1 January 2025. Issuing mandatory from 1 January 2027 for companies whose prior-year turnover exceeded €800,000, and for everyone from 1 January 2028 |
| Romania | Every invoice, B2B and B2C, goes to RO e-Factura within 5 working days of issue (since 1 January 2026) |
| EU (ViDA) | Digital reporting for intra-EU B2B transactions from 1 July 2030 |

<Callout type="warn">
Check every date against the official source before you plan around it. Mandates are amended often, and thresholds differ by country.
</Callout>

## The step ordinary tools miss

Sending an invoice is the easy part. Knowing what happened to it is the hard part. Each invoice moves through sent, accepted, rejected, corrected and resent, and a rejection has to be fixed inside a legal window. A task-based tool sees every send as a separate run, keeps no memory of where an invoice stands, and charges you again for every invoice, every month.

## How it works in PROCESIO

1. Your ERP hands over invoices through a [Webhook](/webhooks), or a [Schedule](/schedules) collects them at intervals.
2. A [Decisional](/how-to/decisional) routes each invoice by country to a [subprocess](/how-to/call-subprocess) for that jurisdiction. Each country's format and network live in their own subprocess, so adding a country means adding one subprocess and leaving the others alone.
3. A [Data Store](/data-store) keeps the state of every invoice. [Query Store](/query-store) answers questions such as "which invoices from last week are still not accepted?"
4. Rejections take the [Error Path](/error-path) into a correction queue. [Delay](/how-to/delay) and scheduled retries keep trying while the legal window is still open.
5. The final status is written back to the ERP, so finance sees it where they already work.
6. A [form](/forms-tasks-designer) with a [Data Table](/data-table) gives the tax team one view across all countries, with the rejection reason next to each invoice.

## What you get

- One place that knows the state of every invoice in every country.
- A correction queue with the network's own rejection message, instead of a search through inboxes.
- A structure that takes the next mandate as one more branch of the same process.
- A cost that follows processing time, however many invoices you send.
